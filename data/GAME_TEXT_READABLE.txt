    --- MISSIONS ---
        [moon]
            general: Listen up. We are going to the Moon to hear it shake.
            purpose: Find out what is inside the Moon by listening to moonquakes.
            why: Quakes travel through different rock in different ways. A network of seismometers works like an X-ray of the Moon's insides.
            tradeoff: The lunar night lasts about two Earth weeks with no sunlight. A bigger battery keeps the instruments alive, but it is heavy and costs money.
            based on: Apollo astronauts left seismometers on the Moon. They recorded moonquakes until 1977.
        [mars]
            general: Listen up. We are going to Mars to find water ice.
            purpose: Find water ice under the ground on Mars and send the data home.
            why: Ice could give future astronauts water, air and rocket fuel. It also tells us how the climate of Mars changed.
            tradeoff: The ice drill is heavy and uses a lot of power. Every kilogram you add must still fit on the rocket.
            based on: Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter (launched 2005) mapped Mars from orbit. The Phoenix lander (2008) dug up real water ice.
        [venus]
            general: Listen up. We are going to Venus to chase its storms.
            purpose: Study the fast storms in the thick clouds of Venus, from orbit and with a balloon.
            why: Venus is almost as big as Earth, but a runaway greenhouse effect made it the hottest planet. Learning why helps us understand our own climate.
            tradeoff: The balloon and the cloud camera both need power and money, and the radio must be strong enough to send it all home.
            based on: The Soviet VEGA balloons floated in the clouds of Venus in 1985. NASA's Magellan mapped Venus with radar.
    source: moon (Apollo seismometers until 1977, moonquakes show the inside): https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/nmc/experiment/display.action?id=1971-063C-01 and https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/missions/farside-seismic-suite/; lunar night 14 Earth days: https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/lrolitho7temperaturevariation27may2014.pdf; mars (ice for drinking water, air and rocket fuel): https://www.nasa.gov/solar-system/planets/mars/nasa-is-locating-ice-on-mars-with-this-new-map/; MRO launched 2005: https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/nmc/spacecraft/display.action?id=2005-029A; Phoenix found water ice: https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/nasa-phoenix-mars-lander-confirms-frozen-water/; venus (runaway greenhouse, hottest planet): https://science.nasa.gov/venus/venus-facts/; VEGA balloons 1985: https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/nmc/spacecraft/display.action?id=1984-125F; Magellan radar map: https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/magellan.html
