You are helping a student Video Editor make a 30-second demo video for a NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026 game.
The video goes on our project submission page. Judges watch it to understand the game fast. Help me plan the
shots, write the on-screen text, and time the edit. I record all the footage myself from the game, so tell me
exactly what to record.

## The game

- Name: "How Did We Get Here?" Made in Godot. Plays in a web browser.
- Challenge: "Space Mission Design Game". Players set a mission goal, design a spacecraft, pick science
  instruments (the payload), choose a launch vehicle (rocket) and an orbit, manage six resources (budget, mass,
  power, communications, data, mission lifetime), fly the mission and see how each choice changed the result.
- Story: the chief mission designer quits, and the General puts a random person in charge. The player picks who
  they are, and that sets the difficulty: The Assistant Scientist (easy), The Janitor (medium), The Student
  (hard). An inner voice talks to the player, and it only knows what that character knows.
- Missions: the Moon (listen for moonquakes), Mars (find ice), Venus (map the storms).
- Flow: Start menu > pick a character > pick a mission > briefing > Mission School (the six resources) >
  Mission Design Lab (a 3D room: walk to pedestals, the chosen part floats over a hologram, two big screens show
  the choice and the six resources) > Review > three build minigames (type part names, hammer, fuel) > 3D launch
  with timed presses > walk around the spacecraft during the flight and fix problems > landing > do the science
  on the planet > Mission Report with a score, a checklist, and what went wrong and why.
- What is new (our big idea): "Other mission design games assume the player is an expert. In our game, what the
  character knows is the difficulty, and the character learns from failure."
  - Three voices, one decision: at the same choice, the Assistant Scientist gives the real term and a real
    number, the Janitor gives an everyday comparison, and the Student asks a question about the trade-off.
  - The voice learns: when a mission fails, the character earns the word for what went wrong (for example
    "payload capacity") in a Notebook, with the real numbers from that run. After that, the inner voice uses
    the word.
- Real NASA numbers are in the game (real rocket costs and lift, real instrument masses, real planet maps).

## Rules I must follow

- 30 seconds total. Judges may watch with the sound off, so every key idea needs on-screen text.
- No real names, faces or voices of any team member (we are under 18). No photos of people. Use the game's own
  characters and text only. Music must be free to use (or the game's own sounds).
- Do not claim things the game does not do. Do not invent NASA facts.

## What I need from you

1. A shot list for 30 seconds: for each shot, the time (for example 0:00-0:03), what I record in the game, and
   the on-screen text (short, big, max 8 words).
2. The order should tell a story: the problem (the designer quit, you are in charge), who you are (three
   characters), the design choice (the Design Lab, the six resources), the twist (the same choice, different
   voices), the failure and the Notebook word, the launch and the report, and a closing line with the game's name.
3. Recording tips: record at 1280x720 or bigger, hide the mouse, stay still for 2 seconds at the start and end of
   each clip so I can cut cleanly.
4. A 15-second backup cut in case 30 seconds is too long.

These are the clips our team already planned (about 20 seconds); build around them:
1. Assistant Scientist at the rocket choice: the inner voice says "Payload capacity is the most mass a launch
   vehicle can lift..." (4 s)
2. The Janitor at the same rocket choice: "You can't fit a couch in a tiny car..." (4 s)
3. The Mission Report after a failed run, with the amber card "New word in your notebook" (4 s)
4. Press N: "The Janitor's Notebook, 1 of 10 words earned" (3 s)
5. Back at the rocket choice, the Janitor now says "Now I know the word: ..." (4 s)
Good extra shots: the 3D launch countdown and liftoff, walking around the spacecraft during the flight, the planet
landing, the Design Lab with the hologram.

Ask me questions if anything is unclear before you write the final shot list.
