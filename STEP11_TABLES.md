Also found: MRO "weighed 2,180 kilograms (4,806 pounds) at launch, including fuel"
([NSSDC](https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/nmc/spacecraft/display.action?id=2005-029A): "The
maximum spacecraft mass is 2180 kg, which includes 1149 kg of propellants").

**⚠ Two problems for the game (a team decision is needed, see the questions below):**
1. **Scale.** Real lifts are 2,300–4,000 kg. Today the game's spacecraft is about
   1,000 kg plus 100–200 kg of orbit fuel, so with real lifts every build would fit.
   Fix: make the orbit fuel real-sized too. The real MRO carried **1,149 kg of
   propellant**, and 1,031 kg + 1,149 kg = 2,180 kg fits on the Atlas V 401
   (2,315 kg), just like the real mission. The orbit-fuel amounts would then be a
   written game-design choice scaled to MRO's real propellant.
2. **Falcon 9 is the cheapest AND lifts the most** (that is true in real life:
   reusable rockets made launches cheaper). In the game, everyone would pick it, and
   the rocket choice would stop being a trade-off. Options: (a) use Falcon 9 anyway
   and keep the trade-off in the launch timing; (b) replace "small" with the retired
   **Delta II 7925** (it launched Phoenix to Mars), but I did not find a reliable
   NASA cost or Mars-lift number for it; (c) keep Falcon 9 but use its reusable lift
   (I did not find a NASA number).

## Part B. Game rules for the team to pass (no NASA number exists)

Each one becomes "Game-design choice, passed by the team (date)":

- `minigames.json`: every timer, target and scoring number.
- `timing.json`: the launch moments, window tightness, ring timing, miss costs and
  the structure check.
- `events.json` flight: the event timing, thresholds (computer, spare power, fuel,
  lunar night, heat), data lost, lifetime lost and the build-quality rules.
- `destinations.json`: orbit fuel amounts (scaled to MRO's real 1,149 kg, see A2),
  arrival hold/send timings, data_gb, report data targets, and the Moon/Venus limits
  other than the budget.
- `game_data.json`: the new part cards (A5), the medium antenna, the structure check,
  and the Moon/Venus limits.
